About the studio

A single room, a few hundred metres from the sea

Kiln & Tide is run by Nia Faulkner out of a converted net loft in Porthkerris, Cornwall. She trained at Falmouth, then spent three years assisting a production potter in Devon before setting up on her own in 2019.

Every piece is thrown, trimmed and glazed by hand, one at a time. The clay is a Cornish stoneware body; the glazes are mixed and tested in small batches, most of them named after conditions on the water that morning — low tide, storm swell, slack water.

There is one wheel, one kiln, and no assistants. That’s a limit on how much gets made — and the reason nothing here looks machine-perfect.

How it’s made

From clay to kiln

Four stages, roughly three weeks, depending on how the weather treats the drying racks.

01

Wedge

Cornish stoneware clay is wedged by hand to remove air and even out moisture before it touches the wheel.

02

Throw

Each form is thrown, then trimmed the next day once it’s firmed up enough to hold a clean foot ring.

03

Glaze

Bisque-fired pieces are dipped or brushed in small-batch glazes, tested for how they pool and break over texture.

04

Fire

A slow 12-hour firing to 1,260°C, then two days cooling in the kiln before anything comes out.
