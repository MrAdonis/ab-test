"A drum roaster with no computer attached isn't a limitation. It's the entire point."

Most roasters our size switched to electric drums with software profiling years ago — load a curve, walk away, get the same batch every time. We kept the gas flame and the cast iron. It means every batch is slightly different, and it means someone has to stand in front of the drum for the full eleven minutes, listening for the crack instead of watching a graph tell them it happened. That's the trade we make on purpose.

Four stages, one person, every batch.

01 / 0:00

Charge

204°C

Drum loaded, flame lit beneath cast iron. Cold beans drop the internal temperature fast — this dip is normal and unavoidable with a manual profile.

02 / 4:30

Yellowing

160°C

Grassy smell fades, sugars start to brown. The roaster adjusts flame by color and smell — no set curve to follow.

03 / 9:15

First Crack

196°C

Moisture vents audibly. From here the roaster's ears matter more than the probe — every second past this point is a choice.

04 / 11:40

Drop

218°C

Beans hit the cooling tray. Average loss across a batch runs 14.2%, logged by hand in the roast book.
